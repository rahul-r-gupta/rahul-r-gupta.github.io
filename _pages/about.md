Hello! 

I am an applied economist specializing in labor and urban economics and the economics of innovation. My research examines how firm structure, competitive behavior, and industrial policy shape regional labor markets, the geography of innovation, and long-run patterns of economic inequality across U.S. cities and regions.

A central focus of my work is the economics of large firms and place-based policy. Governments at all levels increasingly deploy large subsidies to attract major employers, including manufacturing plants, R&D facilities, and headquarters, as tools of regional development. My research asks whether these investments deliver on their promises: who actually gets the jobs created by large firm entry, where those workers come from, and how the competitive dynamics of local labor markets shift as dominant employers expand their footprint.

A parallel line of research examines how firm competition shapes the spatial organization of innovation. Large incumbent firms do not just employ workers, they restructure the markets around them, crowding out or catalyzing local entrepreneurship, redirecting inventor flows, and concentrating innovative activity in ways that have lasting consequences for regional economic trajectories. I study these dynamics to understand what forces, including rising housing costs and internal labor market frictions, determine where new technologies emerge and which places get left behind.

In my work, I employ rigorous causal econometrics and panel methods on Big Data. I have extensive experience using restricted-access U.S. Census administrative microdata such as the Longitudinal Business Database, LEHD, Long Form Census, and the American Community Survey as a Special Sworn Status researcher with the U.S. Census Bureau. 

As of August 2026, I am joining NERA in Washington, D.C., to focus on expert analysis in antitrust and competition matters. Previously, I was an Assistant Professor of Strategy, Economics, and Public Policy at Georgetown University's McDonough School of Business. I earned my Ph.D. in business economics and strategy from Duke University, my M.A. in economics from Boston University, and B.S. and B.A. degrees in finance, economics, and mathematics from Indiana University.
